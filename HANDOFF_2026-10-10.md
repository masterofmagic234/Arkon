## Movement and camera

- Forward is +Z, right is +X. Steering, chassis heading, travel heading and camera projection use that convention.
- Vehicle world-X motion is independent of the centreline. The car is no longer carried through a bend when the driver does not steer.
- Heading and travel respond to steering with speed-dependent steering range and lateral grip. Their difference is slip.
- Speed is total speed; forward progress accounts for lateral travel.
- Stationary steering turns the front wheels without translating or yawing the chassis.
- Braking has priority over throttle and can stop the car.
- Asphalt width, edge projection, car contact, props, AI and light pools share the same world projection. Each road edge has its own camera depth when yawed.
- Off-road speed loss starts after the vehicle/contact allowance. Full throttle cannot cancel the shoulder penalty, and the car can drive back out.
- Top speed remains 219 km/h.
- The Level 2 director owns RaceCameraState. Camera yaw follows the actual nose; camera boom position has inertia. Roll, horizon response and speed widening are shared.
- The model viewport consumes the same camera state and roll. Godot rear-view 3D uses -X screen-right, so yaw/steering are converted once to the opposite sign. It has no independent chase controller. Focal length, camera height and position match the main ground projection.

## 240SX presentation

Source asset remains res://240_sx_nfs_pro_street.glb.
Race240SXRig separates the original paired tire/rim geometry at load time into four wheels with real centres. Wheel geometry is cached and retains source UVs/materials. The GLB itself is not replaced.

- All wheels spin from accumulated travelled distance divided by their radius.
- Only the front wheels steer; stopped wheels stop spinning.
- Chassis roll and pitch respond to steering/acceleration/braking.
- Front lamp surfaces emit warm white light; soft light pools are drawn on the pseudo-3D ground in the nose direction.
- Rear lights have dim running emission and brighter red brake emission, including while the brake is held at rest.
- A soft projected contact shadow supports grounding.
- The transparent render window stays separate from speed-dependent visible car scale.

## User feedback and vehicle revision

The user liked the main camera and handling; requested a grounded nose, neutral front wheels, believable acceleration/automatic shifts, generated engine audio and tire sound/marks. Preserve that successful steering and main-camera behavior.

Grounding/neutral-wheel commit: 39b50582fd3238688c409b1b44f1ac45d3851ffe. Both its Android workflows were verified successful: 38082352522 and 38082349747.

- Source front tires/rims/brake parts have 25 degrees of steering baked into geometry. Rig removes this before rolling; calipers steer without spinning.
- Tire radii/centres are seated on one plane. Sprung body has its own BodySuspension pivot; acceleration/roll no longer raise the tires.
- Original model-only 25-degree camera pitch did not match the pseudo-3D road plane. Corrected frustum matches all four contact patches, including the Godot coordinate conversion. Automated contact error tolerance is 0.1 pixels.
- `scripts/race_powertrain.gd` supplies actual gear, RPM, engine load, torque/converter, rolling/aero drag, RPM-triggered four-speed automatic up/downshifts and kickdown. This is a tuned four-cylinder profile, not a claim to factory-spec 240SX performance. Lateral handling/main camera algorithms are retained.
- Flat-road full-throttle validation: 0-100 = 8.067 s, 0-200 = 39.25 s, 219 cap reached around 69 s; gears 1-4 with three upshifts, RPM drops and short torque interruption. Braking has priority over throttle and about 0.9g maximum deceleration.
- HUD reads the actual gear. Eight original offline-generated PCM engine loops blend four RPM bands and load/coast states. Actual shifts change RPM/torque and trigger a subdued drivetrain transient. Tire friction is a separate loop driven by braking/corner load; stopped tires are silent. All respect GameState SFX mute/volume and scene lifetime.
- `tools/generate_vehicle_audio.py` reproducibly synthesizes the shipped WAVs with NumPy. No external recordings; no Python/DSP runs on the device.
- `scripts/race_skid_marks.gd` stores world-space rubber segments with unwrapped lap progress, distance-based samples, a 384-segment cap and 22-second expiry. Renderer projects/clips them on asphalt; marks do not slide with the camera or draw through the HUD.
